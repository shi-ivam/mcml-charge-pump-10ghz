3.1  Operation of the proposed charge pump
3.1.1  Idle condition “UP = DN = 0”
When both Up and Down signals from PFD are 0 called 
idle condition, then  S1,  S2,  S3 and  S4 switches in the primary 
charge pump becomes OFF and  M3,  M4 becomes ON but 
 S5 and  S6 becomes OFF hence no transition takes place  CL 
remains in the previous state.
3.1.2  Charging condition “UP = 1 and DN = 0”
Figure  4 shows the operation and transient response of 
charging condition. At this condition,  S2,  S4 and  S6 switches 
becomes ON and  S1,  S3 and  S5 becomes OFF. Initially when 
the charge pump receives input,  S2 PMOS transistor becomes 
ON and  S1 NMOS transistor becomes  OFF. The transmission 
gate switches  S4 becomes ON and  S3 becomes OFF. It trans-
mits the charge from  S2 to the output node via  S4. At the same 
time,  S6 PMOS transistor becomes ON and  S5 NMOS transis-
tor becomes OFF. The transistor  S6 is connected to  VDD via 
the PMOS load, when the input UP = 1and UP =0,  S6 PMOS 
becomes ON. This transfers the charge to the output node. 
Hence the load capacitor is charged to  VDD. The capacitor 
charging waveform is shown in Fig. 6a.
To achieve fast locking condition, and avoid transient 
response delay both charge pumps are processed simulta-
neously. Because of the load capacitor  CL is in charging 
condition the output voltage  Vop swings up to a certain level 
of  VDD. The simulated results shows that proposed charge 
pump swings upto 980 mV for 1 V supply.

3.1.4  Sizing of the transistors
In the proposed charge pump design, the transistors are 
sized based on the analysis performed with the power, delay 
and noise for high frequency applications of PLL. Opti-
mal transistor sizing involves, finding the balance between 
speed, power and noise becomes a challenging one. Larger 
transistors can offer faster switching but might consume 
more power during the state changes. Smaller transis-
tors generally exhibit lower parasitic capacitance, thereby 
reducing the impact of thermal noise, a major contributor 
to output noise and phase noise. So based on the simula-
tion results obtained from the trial and error method of the 
proposed charge pump, the transistor sizing is fixed with 
the length of 45 nm and with the optimized width for better 
power, delay and noise management. Figure 7a–c illustrates
