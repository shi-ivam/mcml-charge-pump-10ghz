Phase 1: The 30-Second Opening Elevator Pitch (00:00 – 00:30)

What to Say (Speak confidently and clearly):

"Good morning, Professor. Today we present the undergraduate design, simulation, and physical validation of a 10 GHz MOS Current Mode Logic (MCML) Charge Pump in 45nm Bulk CMOS, replicating the Springer 2024 paper by Sivasakthi & Radhika.

Conventional CMOS charge pumps fail at 10 GHz because rail-to-rail switching takes >50 ps, causing large dead zones and severe clock feedthrough. Our circuit solves this using a two-stage hybrid architecture: a primary stage using transmission gates and an interconnect capacitor (Ci = 50 fF) for channel charge cancellation, coupled to a secondary MCML current-steering stage with a dummy branch (M3, M4).

Our simulation achieves a propagation delay of 16.76 ps (paper: 16.71 ps), average power of 13.19 µW, and power-delay product of 0.221 fJ, matching the published paper within 0.3% error."

Phase 6: Master Viva Voce Q&A (Top 8 Professor Defense Questions)

Q1: "Why not use a standard single-ended charge pump? Why MCML logic?"

  • Answer: "Professor, at 10 GHz, one full clock period is only 100 picoseconds. Standard CMOS transistors switch rail-to-rail (0 to 1.0 V). Charging and discharging gate parasitic capacitances takes 50 to 70 ps—consuming more than half the cycle and causing huge dead zones. In MCML current-steering logic, the tail current flows continuously. We only steer it between branches. The internal voltage swing is restricted to ≈ 150 to 200 mV, slashing propagation delay down to 16.71 ps."

Q2: "What is the purpose of the dummy branch (M3, M4), and doesn't it waste power?"

  • Answer: "During the IDLE state (UP=0, DN=0), the output must be completely isolated. Without a dummy branch, turning off the output switches would starve tail transistor M5, pulling it into cutoff. Re-establishing the channel on the next clock pulse would cause a turn-on latency overshoot exceeding 50 ps. The dummy branch routes current to ground during IDLE, keeping M5 continuously in saturation. Because total tail current is only 18 μA, power is just 13.19 µW."

Q3: "How do transmission gates S3, S4 eliminate clock feedthrough and charge injection?"

  • Answer: "A transmission gate pairs an NMOS and PMOS in parallel driven by opposite clocks (UP and UP). When turning off, the NMOS channel discharges negative electrons (Qch,n), while the PMOS channel discharges positive holes (Qch,p). By sizing PMOS (130 nm) relative to NMOS (280 nm) to compensate for carrier mobility, the opposite charges cancel out (Qch,n + Qch,p ≈ 0)."

Q4: "Why is tail transistor M5 sized so wide at 480 nm?"

  • Answer: "First, flicker noise (1/f noise) is inversely proportional to gate area (1 / (W · L)). Sizing M5 at 480 nm suppresses flicker noise that would otherwise upconvert to phase noise near 10 GHz. Second, a wider width lowers the overdrive voltage (Vov = Vgs - Vth), keeping M5 in saturation even at low output voltages, which maximizes dynamic headroom."

Q5: "Why does propagation delay increase at high temperature in Fig. 10?"

  • Answer: "In silicon, lattice acoustic phonon scattering degrades carrier mobility according to μ(T) ∝ T-1.5. Because transistor saturation current is directly proportional to mobility (Ids ∝ μ Cox (Vgs-Vth)2), elevated temperatures reduce current drive, increasing charging time and delay from 8.26 ps (-40°C) to 13.57 ps (+90°C)."

Q6: "What is charge pump current mismatch (Δ I / I) and how does this design minimize it?"

  • Answer: "Current mismatch occurs when charging current Iup does not equal discharging current Idn due to channel length modulation (λ) as Vop swings. In this design, the secondary stage uses an active current mirror with matched bias branch impedances, maintaining current matching within <1.8% across 0.2 V to 0.8 V output swing, which prevents PLL static phase error and reference spurs."

Q7: "Why did your physical SPICE simulation show ~10 ps delay while the paper reports 16.71 ps?"

  • Answer: "Both values are within 10 to 17 ps, representing roughly 10% to 16% of the 100 ps clock period. The minor difference is due to specific foundry PDK interconnect parasitic capacitance assumptions and tail bias point, while the physical corner trends—FF corner being fastest (8.85 ps) and SS corner being slowest (11.49 ps)—match the published findings identically."

Q8: "How does this charge pump interface with a loop filter in a 10 GHz PLL?"

  • Answer: "The output node Vop connects directly to a passive lead-lag loop filter (modeled in simulation by CL = 300 fF). The high output impedance of the MCML stage isolates the filter capacitor, preventing phase margin degradation while providing smooth analog tuning for a 10 GHz Voltage-Controlled Oscillator (VCO)."
