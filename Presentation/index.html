Theory & Foundations

What is a CMOS Charge Pump?

Inductorless DC–DC Voltage Boosting via Transistor-Controlled Charge Redistribution
02 / 10
Core Definition & Operation

A CMOS charge pump is a switched-capacitor DC–DC converter used to generate an output voltage higher than the available supply voltage without magnetic inductors.

  • No Inductors Required: The circuit uses transistor switches and capacitors only.
  • MOS Transistor Switches: NMOS and PMOS switches driven by non-overlapping clock phases.
  • Pumping Capacitors: Intermediate charge reservoirs that store and elevate packet energy.
  • Multi-Stage Cascading: Sequential transfer stages multiply the achievable DC level.
Fundamental Working Principle
Capacitive Charge Storage
$$Q = C \cdot V$$
During one clock phase, the capacitor is charged. During the next clock phase, its stored voltage is shifted upward and transferred:
$$V_{OUT} > V_{IN}$$
IC Context: Used inside ICs for memories (Flash/EEPROM wordline bias), power management, and internal substrate voltage generation.

Transistor-Level Design Elements

The charge pump is created using individual device primitives in Cadence Virtuoso. No prebuilt converter IC or macromodel is used:

  • NMOS and PMOS Transistors: Custom channel aspect ratios ($W/L$).
  • Integrated Capacitors: Metal-Insulator-Metal (MIM) and MOS capacitor banks.
  • Clock Drivers: Sized CMOS inverter buffers for non-overlapping phases.
  • Input Signals: UP/DN voltage sources with adjustable timing.
Key Transistor Design Variables: Wn, Wp, L
ON-Resistance ($R_{ON}$):
Dictates conduction drop and charge-transfer speed.
Parasitic Capacitances:
$C_{gs}, C_{gd}, C_{db}$ cause clock feedthrough and power loss.
Body Effect ($\gamma$):
Shifts threshold voltage $V_{th}(V_{SB})$ across cascaded stages.
Power Dissipation:
Dynamic switching loss $P = f_{clk} C_{tot} V^2$ scales with width.

How the Charge Pump Circuit Works

A simple view of how the UP and DN signals control the output voltage

What This Circuit Does
  • S1–S4 work like electronic ON/OFF switches.
  • Ci moves a small amount of electrical charge during each operation.
  • CL stores and smooths the voltage at the Vop output.
Three Easy Operating States
UP
The circuit adds charge, so the output voltage rises.
DN
The circuit removes charge, so the output voltage falls.
No signal
The capacitor helps keep the output nearly steady.
