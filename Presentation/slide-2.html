What is a CMOS Charge Pump?

PLL control-voltage adjustment through transistor-controlled charge redistribution
02 / 10
Core Definition & Operation

In this PLL application, the CMOS charge pump is a switched-capacitor control-voltage circuit. It converts UP and DN decisions into a Vop adjustment within the supply range, rather than boosting Vop above VDD.

  • Switches S1-S4: NMOS and PMOS paths controlled by UP, DN, and complementary signals.
  • Transfer Capacitor Ci: Temporarily stores a charge packet for controlled redistribution.
  • Output Capacitor CL: Stores charge at Vop and smooths the control voltage.
  • PLL Role: Vop corrects the oscillator frequency through the loop filter or VCO control path.
Fundamental Working Principle
Capacitive Charge Storage
$$Q = C \cdot V$$
During an UP action charge is directed toward CL. During a DN action charge is removed from CL. The control voltage remains within the supply range:
$$0 \leq V_{op} < V_{DD}$$
PLL Context: The phase-frequency detector creates UP/DN decisions. The charge pump converts them into Vop for the PLL control path.
